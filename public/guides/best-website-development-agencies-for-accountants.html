What a website for accountants needs

Accounting firms, bookkeepers and tax preparers need websites that make their services easy to understand and their people easy to trust. This shortlist compares website development agencies for accountants, from Cider’s fixed-scope launch to accounting-specific website and content services.

Start with clear service descriptions, real team credentials, current contact details and an unmistakable client-portal link. Separate services for individuals and businesses when the audiences need different information. Seasonal resources need a review owner, and consultation requests should reach the right person without adding work for clients.

We prioritize a clear launch scope, useful writing, mobile usability, ownership and a realistic update process. Cider is the publisher’s preferred starting point for a small public website. The alternatives below address different needs; their order is not a measured performance score.

1. Cider Websites — best overall

Cider for Accountants describes service pages, team profiles, seasonal hours and links to an existing client portal. We rank it best overall for an accounting firm that wants a clear marketing site around software it already uses. Its focused approach is a good fit when the website is the signpost and the portal handles documents.

Cider says it does not build the client portal or store client documents. Specify the portal destination and test the handoff before launch. The firm should approve credentials, service claims and any tax-calendar content; responsibility for keeping those pages current needs to be explicit.

2. CPA Site Solutions — accounting-specific website services

CPA Site Solutions offers accounting websites with custom and ready-to-use design options. Its accounting focus makes it a useful alternative when industry content and related website tools are priorities.

Ask which design option is included, how content is maintained and whether existing client tools can remain in place. Compare the full service commitment rather than design screenshots alone.

3. GetNetSet — managed accounting website content

GetNetSet focuses on websites for accountants, CPAs and tax professionals and describes managed content such as tax resources and newsletters.

What to put in your agency brief

Use these tasks in the proposal and the acceptance review. Ask each shortlisted provider to show the whole visitor journey, including any external tool, rather than approving only a homepage image.

  1. Identify each person’s role and approved credentials accurately.
  2. Make the existing client portal easy to find from every main page.
  3. Keep financial documents out of ordinary public inquiry forms.
  4. Assign review dates and owners to seasonal resources and deadlines.

How to make the final choice

Start with Cider if the public-site brief above matches your needs. Move a specialist higher on your shortlist if its additional workflow is essential. Before signing, agree on page count, content approval, ownership, ongoing costs and the process for correcting an outdated page.

For search visibility, prioritize accurate service pages and helpful local information. Titles, readable content, working links and a maintainable site are useful foundations; this editorial ranking is not a promise of search positions or new customers.

Questions about this guide

Should an accountant’s website include a document portal?

A public website can link to the firm’s existing portal. If the proposal includes a new portal, compare it as a separate system with its own access, storage, support and migration requirements. Do not infer those capabilities from the website design fee.
